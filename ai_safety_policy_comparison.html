Detailed Dimension Analysis

1. Risk Categorization & Thresholds

  • Anthropic (RSP / ASL): Uses an explicit tiered model modeled after Biosafety Levels (ASL-1 to ASL-4). Each level defines explicit capability thresholds (e.g., ASL-3 involves significantly enhanced CBRN risk or autonomous capability) and mandates corresponding containment/security controls before scaling or deploying models that meet those thresholds.
  • Google (Frontier Safety Commitments & SAIF): Evaluates capability levels against "Critical Capability Thresholds" across key domain areas (CBRN, offensive cyber actions, self-reasoning/persuasion). Models are evaluated prior to and during training milestones.
  • OpenAI (Preparedness Framework): Employs a standardized 4x4 matrix rating risks as Low, Medium, High, or Critical across four primary hazard categories (Cybersecurity, CBRN, Persuasion, and Autonomous Replication).

    • 2. Pause / Stop Triggers

  • Anthropic: Hard commitment to halt scaling or deployment if a model crosses into higher ASL levels (e.g., ASL-3/ASL-4) without meeting the strictly defined organizational and technical safeguards for that level.
  • Google: Evaluates safety at clear gates throughout the training lifecycle; commits to pausing deployment or further capability scaling if required mitigations cannot reliably control risks below critical thresholds.
  • OpenAI: If a model hits a "Critical" risk tier across any category, OpenAI commits to halting further development or scaling of the model until mitigations bring post-mitigation risk down to "High" or lower for deployment.

    • 3. Governance & Board Oversight

  • Anthropic: Features an innovative legal governance structure anchored by the Long-Term Benefit Trust, an independent body with authority to influence corporate governance and board selection to protect the safety mission.
  • Google: Relies on a centralized Responsible AI Council combined with deep integration into DeepMind’s specialized safety units, backed by external advisory alignment groups.
  • OpenAI: Governance centers on the Safety and Security Committee, which directly advises the Board of Directors and holds review authority over major deployment decisions and technical safety readiness.

    • 4. Red-Teaming & Independent Auditing

  • Anthropic: Emphasizes independent, third-party pre-deployment red-teaming and safety audits, combined with automated alignment methods (Constitutional AI).
  • Google: Integrates internal red-teaming via the Responsibility & Safety team with the Secure AI Framework (SAIF) and extensive external domain-expert evaluations.
  • OpenAI: Operates a dedicated External Red Teaming Network comprising specialized domain experts to continuously pressure-test models before release.

    • Strategic Takeaways

    1. Precision vs. Flexibility: Anthropic’s ASL framework and OpenAI’s Preparedness Framework provide explicit, formal quantitative/categorical stop conditions, whereas Google’s policy combines capability gates with continuous review boards and enterprise security integration (SAIF).

    2. Governance Independence: Anthropic relies on a structurally independent corporate trust (Long-Term Benefit Trust) to maintain safety incentives, whereas OpenAI and Google operate through board-level committees and internal governance councils.

    3. Consensus on Frontier Threat Vectors: All three leading institutions agree on the four core frontier risk vectors requiring strict evaluation prior to model release: CBRN capabilities, offensive cyber operations, autonomous replication/agentic behavior, and persuasion/deception.
